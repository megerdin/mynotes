Chapter One — The Arrival

The Lantern at North Point

It was just after seven when the first light appeared beyond the

 headland. The town of North Point was still quiet, and the sea moved gently beneath a pale morning sky.
Mara had learned that ordinary places often kept extraordinary secrets. She walked slowly along the harbour wall, listening to the gulls and the soft rhythm of the water.
On the previous evening she had found a small brass lantern in the attic of her

grandfather's house. It was old, but carefully made, with a narrow glass window and a tiny mark shaped like a star.

She carried the lantern with her now. It was not lit, yet it seemed strangely warm in her hands. Every few steps she stopped and looked towards the northern cliffs, wondering why the object had been hidden for so many years.

A folded note inside the lantern had contained only one sentence: "When the light points north, follow the road that the tide cannot reach."

Mara had no idea what the sentence meant. She only knew that her grandfather had never written riddles without a reason. That thought was enough to keep her moving.

At the end of the harbour she noticed something she had never seen before: a narrow iron gate between two stone walls. Beyond it was a path leading inland, exactly where the morning shadow of the lighthouse fell.

She opened the gate. The hinge gave a quiet creak, and th

e lantern suddenly became brighter. Not with a flame, but with a faint golden glow beneath the glass.

For a moment Mara considered turning back. Then she smiled. Whatever waited beyond the gate, she suspected the journey mattered more than the answer.

She stepped onto the path and began to walk. Behind her, North Point slowly woke to another ordinary day. Ahead, the road disappeared between the trees.
